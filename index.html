About Me

I am a Ph.D. student at the School of Public Affairs, Zhejiang University (2024-2029). My research leverages large-scale administrative microdata to explore issues in empirical microeconomics, with a particular focus on the economics of education, social mobility, and urban-rural inequality.

Prior to joining ZJU, I received my Bachelor of Economics from Minzu University of China, where I graduated ranked 1st in the Top Talent Innovation Program.

Working Papers

  • Is It Hard for Poor Families to Foster Talented Children? Career Development Differentials Between Urban and Rural College Graduates
    Focus: Investigates educational inequality, social mobility, and the intergenerational transmission of socioeconomic status in developing contexts.
    Methodology: Leverages unique, large-scale administrative microdata to analyze the impact of urban-rural origins on long-term labor market outcomes.
  • "Escaping Rural Roots" to "Returning to Hometowns": The Impact of Urban-Rural Disparities on Spatial Mobility Patterns of ZJU Graduates
    Focus: Examines how top-tier educational attainment mitigates or exacerbates spatial inequality.
    Methodology: Employs comprehensive micro-level graduate tracking data from Zhejiang University to evaluate spatial sorting behaviors.
  • The Impact of Overqualification Perception on Work Engagement Among Young Civil Servants in China
